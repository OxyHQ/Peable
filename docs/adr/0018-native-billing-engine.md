# ADR 0018 — Orbit runs its own billing engine

**Status:** accepted; Phase 4b as built (below); the proxy migration's tooling built, its live cutover pending.
**Supersedes, when implemented:** ADR 0010 (card rail) and ADR 0013's renewal hold.
**Date:** 2026-11-03.

## Context

Card subscriptions today are Stripe Billing objects reached through Peable:
Stripe Checkout in `mode: subscription`, the Stripe Billing Portal pinned to a
`bpc_` configuration with plan changes disabled, invoices generated by Stripe and
read back. Peable adds real value on top — tax decided by Peable Tax, renewal
drafts held until repriced (ADR 0013), signed final-invoice authorities (ADR
0012), leased operation recovery — but each of those exists to correct an object
Peable does not own. ADR 0013 records the cost directly: prorations, discounts and
plan changes are not priced, so they are refused.

FairCoin billing is already native (`faircoinBillingService.ts`): Peable creates
the periods and invoices itself and collects each one with a PaymentIntent.

## Decision

1. **Orbit owns Products, Prices, Coupons, Promotion codes, Subscriptions,
   Invoices and Credit notes** as native rows (ADR 0016) with a native state
   machine. The provider is used only to *collect* an invoice: an off-session
   PaymentIntent against a saved PaymentMethod on the card rail, an explicit payer
   checkout on the FairCoin rail.
2. **Tax is computed before an invoice finalizes**, by Peable Tax, for every line.
   There is no provider-generated draft to hold and reprice, so ADR 0013's hold
   does not apply to native subscriptions; its tax-reversal rules (refunds are
   reversals netted into the same report rows) apply unchanged.
3. **Time is injected.** The engine never reads the wall clock directly; it reads a
   clock that is real in live mode and may be a test clock (`clock_`) in test mode,
   so a merchant can advance a subscription through trials, renewals and dunning
   in seconds.
4. **Every state change goes through one transition function per object**, writing
   the row, the event (ADR 0014 D4) and the webhook enqueue in one transaction,
   with a compare-and-swap on the source status — the `transitionIntent` rules,
   applied to subscriptions and invoices.
5. **Scope of the first engine:** recurring and one-time prices, quantity, trials,
   proration (`create_prorations | none | always_invoice`), plan changes, cancel
   now / at period end, pause collection, coupons and promotion codes, dunning
   with a configurable retry schedule and final state, invoice numbering per
   merchant, PDF and hosted invoice page. Metered usage, subscription schedules and
   quotes follow.
6. **Entitlements are part of billing.** Features attached to products resolve to
   active entitlements per customer, which is the authoritative access answer an
   app such as Mention asks — including access granted by another product (Mention
   Plus through Oxy One) without a second purchase.
7. **The Stripe Billing proxy is removed when the engine lands** (ADR 0016 §5).
   Subscriptions created through it are migrated onto native objects —
   customer, saved payment method, subscription with its current period — and the
   provider subscription is cancelled at the end of its paid period with no
   second charge. ADR 0010 and ADR 0013's draft hold are superseded for card
   billing at that point; their tax-reversal rules carry over.

## Consequences

- Plan changes, prorations and discounts become supported instead of refused.
- Dunning, receipts and invoice emails are Orbit's responsibility (sent through
  Oxy's outbound mail relay), not the provider's defaults.
- Saved PaymentMethods and SetupIntents (Orbit payments core) are a prerequisite
  for card renewals on the native engine.

## As built (Phase 4b)

The engine lives beside the Stripe-Billing proxy, not instead of it: native
objects under their own routes, the proxy's `/v1/billing/*` untouched until its
consumers move (§7, the plan below). Code: `services/billingEngine/*`,
`db/billingEngine/*`, `db/schema/billingEngine.ts` (+ `testClocks.ts`,
`meters.ts`), `routes/billingEngine/*`; schema decisions in `CONVENTIONS.md`
§Billing engine.

### Decisions taken while building it

1. **One transition function per object** (`transitions.ts`):
   `transitionSubscription` / `transitionInvoice` check the state machine
   (`stateMachines.ts`), write with a CAS on the status read, record the event
   (`customer.subscription.created|updated|deleted|paused|resumed`,
   `invoice.finalized|paid|voided|marked_uncollectible`) and refresh the
   customer's entitlement summary, in the caller's transaction.
2. **The payment hook.** `transitionIntent` calls `onInvoicePaymentTransition`
   inside the PaymentIntent's own transaction, so an invoice is paid (tax
   committed, subscription active again) or failed (attempt counted, retry
   scheduled, `past_due`) atomically with the payment. A card intent is linked
   to its invoice BEFORE it is confirmed, so the hook always finds it;
   `confirmCardIntent` (`services/confirmIntent.ts`) is the one server-side
   confirm, shared with `POST /v1/payment_intents/:id/confirm`.
3. **FairCoin is collected by the payer.** The rail follows the currency: a
   `FAIR` subscription or invoice is `send_invoice` (anything else is 400
   `faircoin_cannot_debit`), and each period is paid from the payer's wallet
   through the hosted invoice page's PaymentIntent — explicit renewal, never a
   debit. A fiat-priced, FX-quoted FairCoin sale stays on
   `faircoinBillingService.ts` until it is migrated (below).
4. **Tax before finalize, fail closed** (`invoiceTax.ts`). With
   `automatic_tax.enabled` the customer's location is recorded as fresh Peable
   Tax evidence from their billing address and card country; insufficient
   evidence is `automatic_tax.status: requires_location_inputs`, a provider
   failure `failed` — the invoice stays a draft (retried hourly when
   auto-advancing); it never finalizes untaxed. Uncovered sales are
   `not_registered` (zero, recorded). A subscription's FIRST invoice is priced
   and taxed before anything is written, so a fail-closed create writes
   nothing. Proration credits are not recalculated: their tax is the credited
   line's tax scaled to the share, and once paid they are reversals of that
   line's tax transaction (ADR 0013's rule). Credit notes are reversals too;
   pre-payment notes are reversed when the invoice's tax is committed. Native
   tax transactions carry `rail: 'orbit'`, and `POST
   /v1/tax/transactions/:id/reversals` refuses them (their reversals are
   credit notes).
5. **Numbering is gapless per merchant (and so per mode).** The counter lives in
   `billing_settings` and is taken by one `UPDATE … RETURNING next - 1` in the
   finalize transaction; a rollback returns it. Prefix: eight characters of the
   merchant id unless set. Credit notes are `<invoice number>-CN-nn`.
6. **Time is injected** (`runtime.ts`). `engineNow` answers a test clock's
   `frozen_time` for its customers and the wall clock otherwise; the
   scheduler's claim judges "due" on each row's own clock inside the claim
   statement. Advancing a test clock runs passes for it until nothing on it is
   due, then answers `ready` (Stripe answers `advancing` first; completing
   before answering is the deliberate departure — a test needs no polling).
7. **The scheduler** (`scheduler.ts`) has no job table: subscriptions and
   invoices carry `next_action_at`, claimed with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and a
   lease, worked outside the claim. It is idempotent by the rows themselves:
   renewal invoices are unique per (subscription, reason, period start), every
   status write is a CAS, payment attempts are keyed by invoice and attempt
   number. Started per process in `server.ts`, `.unref()`ed.
8. **Dunning defaults**: retries 1, 3, 5 and 7 days after each failed attempt;
   then the invoice is marked uncollectible and the subscription cancelled;
   invoices due 30 days after finalization; emails on. A provider outage defers
   an attempt an hour without spending one. A `send_invoice` invoice past due
   moves the subscription to `past_due`, and the final action runs once the
   schedule's total length has also passed.
9. **Payment behaviour.** `allow_incomplete` (default) charges the first invoice
   at once; `default_incomplete` leaves its PaymentIntent for the payer
   (`latest_invoice.payment_intent`); `error_if_incomplete` answers 402 — and
   keeps the rows it wrote as `incomplete_expired` with a void invoice rather
   than deleting them (history is never removed). An unpaid first invoice
   expires the subscription after 23 hours (`send_invoice`: at its due date).
10. **Prorations** are by the second, rounded once per line; the credit names
    the line it credits (`credits_line_id`). `always_invoice` and an interval
    change invoice at once (`subscription_update`); `create_prorations` leaves
    pending invoice items for the next renewal; `DELETE` with `invoice_now`
    bills a final `subscription_cancel` invoice, whose negative total becomes
    customer credit.
11. **One discount** per subscription or invoice (`coupon`, `promotion_code` or a
    one-entry `discounts[]`); stacked discounts are refused rather than
    guessed. Redemptions are counted by CAS when the discount is applied.
12. **Entitlements** are the active features granted by products on the
    customer's `trialing`, `active` or `past_due` subscriptions — one per
    feature whatever the number of granting products, so Mention Plus through
    the Plus product or through Oxy One is one entitlement, and buying One never
    asks for Plus. `entitlements.active_entitlement_summary.updated` is recorded
    only when the set moves.
13. **Mail** rides Orbit's ONE transactional email outbox (`services/email/*`,
    Orbit Phase 3): an `invoice` email (a finalized `send_invoice` invoice when
    the billing settings send them, or `POST /v1/invoices/:id/send`) and an
    `invoice_payment_failed` notice (dunning) are OWED in the transaction of the
    change, rendered in the payer's locale with a link to the hosted invoice,
    and sent by the dispatcher through the injected `Mailer`. `invoice.sent` and
    `sent_at` are recorded only when the relay accepted the email; with no
    mailer wired, rows wait and expire — nothing claims a send.
14. **The PDF** is rendered by a dependency-free PDF 1.4 writer (`pdf.ts`):
    standard Helvetica, WinAnsi text, no embedded fonts — a few hundred bytes of
    objects instead of a layout engine in the runtime image.
15. **Checkout's `mode: 'subscription'`** is fulfilled by the engine
    (`checkoutFulfiller.ts`, registered at boot): a session's lines must be
    stored, licensed recurring prices on one currency and interval; at confirm
    the subscription is created `default_incomplete` with the session's
    customer, tax setting and discount (redeemed once, by the session), its
    first invoice finalized (tax before finalize), and the session wraps that
    invoice's PaymentIntent — a card one saving its card for the renewals
    (`setup_future_usage: off_session`). The invoice must ask for exactly the
    session's amount or nothing is charged; the payment hook activates the
    subscription in the completion's transaction.
16. **Search** (`GET /v1/subscriptions/search`, `GET /v1/invoices/search`,
    Orbit Phase 8's one Search API) is a field map in `db/search/searchFields.ts`
    and a registry entry walking `listSubscriptions` / `listInvoices` with the
    compiled predicate, serialized by the lists' own batch renderers. A
    subscription search walks every status: hiding canceled subscriptions is
    the list's default, not the search's.

17. **Adoption** (`adoption.ts`, the migration's entry point, §7 below) inserts
    a subscription in its CURRENT state — status, period, anchor, trial, one
    licensed price, the adopted card — with NO invoice, and records
    `customer.subscription.created` and the entitlement refresh in the
    caller's transaction. Two columns carry what makes it different:
    `provider_billed_until` (a prorating item change before it is refused 400
    `param: proration_behavior` — there is no engine line to credit, and
    charging the new price's remaining time without that credit bills the
    payer twice; `proration_behavior: none` is accepted) and
    `provider_handoff_pending` (the previous provider still renews it: the
    subscription is INERT — `next_action_at` null, no renewal, no trial end,
    and merchant writes answer 409 — until `releaseProviderHandoff`, which
    recomputes the schedule, so a period end already past renews at the next
    pass). While held, `realignHeldPeriod` moves it onto a period the provider
    billed meanwhile; nothing else moves a period from outside the engine.

### Not built yet

Subscription schedules and quotes (as §5 says); `invoice.finalization_failed`;
stacked discounts; FX-quoted (fiat-priced) FairCoin invoices on the engine;
deleting a test clock's customers with it (its subscriptions are cancelled); a
production mail relay adapter (the outbox's `Mailer`); trials in checkout's
subscription mode; the portal's card update needs the provider SDK to expose
setup confirmation in the browser (`@peable.to/sdk`'s `ProviderSdk` type covers
payments only today).

### Migrating the Stripe-Billing proxy onto native objects (§7) — as built

`bun run billing:migrate-proxy -- --mode=test|live` (`services/billing/proxyMigration.ts`,
CLI `proxyMigrationCli.ts`), run as a one-shot task of the release image. Each
proxied subscription (a Stripe `sub_…` bound in `billing_object_bindings`) is
planned on its own, and its record — `billing_proxy_migrations`, unique per
binding — is both the audit (binding, Stripe `sub_…`, native customer, card,
price and subscription, `adopted_at`/`released_at`) and the marker a rerun
skips on. Cohorts are merchants; test mode first.

**The plan reads only** (a dry run plans inside a READ-ONLY transaction, over a
provider holding only Stripe reads, and is the default). Per subscription it
answers `migrate`, `resume` (an `adopted` record), `released`, `skip`
(`ended` at Stripe) or `blocked` with the reason: `status_<s>` (only `active`
and `trialing` move — `incomplete`, `past_due`, `unpaid` are resolved at Stripe
first), `open_invoice` (a draft, open or uncollectible invoice: a renewal in
progress, an ADR 0013 hold included), `items`, `discount`, `cancel_at`,
`collection_paused`, `no_payment_method`, `payment_method_not_attached`,
`price_unmapped` / `price_missing` / `price_mismatch_<field>` (the plan file
maps each bound Stripe price to the Orbit price of the same offer; currency,
amount, interval, usage and `tax_behavior` must be identical), `customer_*`,
`tax_settings_not_active` / `tax_location_missing` (a Peable-Tax-quoted
subscription — its checkout operation holds a tax snapshot — or one Stripe
taxed migrates with `automatic_tax` on, which needs approved settings and the
payer's billing address), `period_mismatch` (Stripe's period is not the
engine's anchored period), `period_ends_soon` (inside `--lead-hours`, default
48 — left for after Stripe's renewal), `platform_account_mismatch`,
`provider_key_missing`. Nothing is guessed.

**Execution, and what a crash between two steps leaves:**

1. **Adopt, one transaction**: the Orbit customer bound to the SAME Stripe
   customer (`customer_provider_bindings`, found or created with
   `metadata.store_id` = the proxy's store reference, email, name and billing
   address copied — no new provider customer), the SAME Stripe card registered
   as its `pm_…` (its default when it had none), the native subscription via
   `adoptSubscription` (current state, no invoice, `metadata.store_id` and
   `plan_id`) and the record, `adopted`. Access is native from this commit.
   The subscription is HELD: Stripe still renews it, the engine cannot.
   *Crash before:* nothing written. *Crash after:* one biller (Stripe), access
   unchanged; the next run resumes at 2.
2. **Release at Stripe**: `cancel_at_period_end: true`, `pause_collection:
   {behavior: void}`, `proration_behavior: none`, `metadata.orbit_subscription`
   = the native `sub_…`, under a key derived from the record and the period.
   No refund, no charge; the paid period ends naturally, and the voided
   collection means a subscriber who undoes the cancellation in a Stripe
   portal session opened before the migration is still never charged again.
   If Stripe renewed while a stuck record waited, the held subscription is
   realigned onto the period Stripe billed first (`realignHeldPeriod`); if
   Stripe ended it without this release, the record stays `adopted` as
   `anomaly:provider_ended_early` for an operator. *Crash after Stripe
   answered:* the next run reads the flags and the marker back and goes to 3
   without writing again.
3. **Release natively, one transaction**: `releaseProviderHandoff` and the
   record `released` (CAS). The engine renews at the period end, off-session
   against the adopted card; a period end that passed while the release waited
   renews at the next pass — late, never twice, never missed.

Every run resumes `adopted` records before migrating anything, and
re-verifies `released` ones until Stripe's period has ended: a release undone
at Stripe is reported (`provider_release_reverted` in a dry run) and
re-asserted. One subscription's failure is reported on its line; the run goes
on. A live `--execute` moves ONE `--cohort` and requires `--confirm-live`
naming it.

**Run in test mode against Stripe's test API (2026-11-04)**: three proxied
subscriptions on a Stripe test clock — active, cancelling, discounted. The dry
run planned two `migrate` and one `blocked: discount` and wrote nothing; the
execution adopted both and Stripe accepted the release
(`cancel_at_period_end` with `pause_collection: void`); a rerun only verified.
Both clocks advanced past the period end: Stripe ended both subscriptions with
their one `subscription_create` invoice (no second charge), the engine renewed
the active one off-session against the migrated card (a succeeded Stripe test
PaymentIntent for the same 599), the cancelling one ended natively and lost its
entitlement, and the blocked one renewed at Stripe untouched.

**The proxy freezes a migrated customer**: `checkout_sessions`,
`portal_sessions` and `cancel_at_period_end` answer 409 `subscription_migrated`
for a customer binding with a record — a new Stripe checkout would be a second
subscription and a Stripe portal could undo the release. Reads (subscription,
invoices, signed invoice authorities of the Stripe-paid periods) keep
answering, and Stripe refunds of those periods keep being reversed by ADR
0013's recorder.

**Consumers switch in the release that migrates their cohort** (§6): a
migrated subscriber's proxy subscription reads `cancel_at_period_end: true`,
so a consumer still reading the proxy would show a cancellation. Each consumer
finds its native customer by `metadata['store_id']` (customer search) and reads
`subscriptions` and `entitlements.activeEntitlements`, which include One →
Plus. As of 2026-11-04: oxy (Oxy One / Mention Plus personal plans) has its
Peable runtime off in production with no Peable price bound; Mercaria's
merchant-plan cohort is configured but reads its subscriptions straight from
Stripe as well; Mention reads Plus from oxy. Production holds no proxy
binding, operation or mirror at all (read-only probe), and the gateway has no
`PEABLE_BILLING_COHORT` or Stripe key in its task definition.

**Remove the proxy** (ADR 0016 §5) once no proxy subscription is left
un-migrated and no consumer calls `/v1/billing/*` other than meters: the
routes, `services/billing/*` (keeping ADR 0013's refund-reversal rules for the
Stripe-paid periods), its contracts and SDK methods; ADR 0010 and ADR 0013's
draft hold marked superseded. The FX-quoted FairCoin rail (`faircoinBillingService.ts`,
no production subscription) is moved or removed in the same step.
